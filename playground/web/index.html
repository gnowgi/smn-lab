smn-playground

One block, many bodies, two habitats. Every creature here is built by snapping together the same unit: a segment, pull-only muscle units and a pair of sensors. Each one is the smn-lab simulation itself, running live in your browser on MuJoCo, with no recorded video.

This browser would not start 3D graphics (WebGL), so you are seeing a flat top view of the same simulation. Reason given: . In Firefox, check that hardware acceleration is on and that WebGL is not disabled in about:config (webgl.disabled).

Each row is one block, head at the top, and time runs left to right. Felt change is how much each block's chemical reading changed. Change caused by the world is what remains after the block subtracts what its own movement should have produced. This is Q1's per-zone reafference, consistency-checked on the bench. Crawl through a still field and the world band stays quiet. Move the food while it crawls, and the world band lights up. Q1 was audited on a linear chemical slope. The food's field is curved, so here the subtraction is approximate. Touch is the contact force on each block's skin. Bend it achieves is how far each joint actually bends, measured against what its rhythm commands. On uneven ground in the terrarium it shrinks on rough patches, where the muscles reach their limit, and swells on slick ones. The rhythm itself does not change: every oscillator still runs at 0.9 Hz. The ground reshapes the wobble only mechanically. Getting the rhythm to answer the ground is an open problem on the bench ledger (S0b, proprioceptive entrainment).

The block

A segment, pull-only muscle units and a sensor on each side. In a chain, two units oppose each other across every joint, and that opposed pair is the CAZ. In the bell, the same units form rings and spokes. No central controller sits above any of it.

What you are watching

No choreography

Each joint runs its own oscillator and exchanges phase messages only with its neighbours. The wave that travels down the body comes out of that local exchange. Nothing scripts the gait.

The habitat completes the motion

The same body in the same rhythm meets water in one tank and ground in the other. In water, sideways drag is much stronger than drag along the body. On ground, belly friction is weight-set and grips sideways. Both resistances are anisotropic, and that turns undulation into forward travel.

The world pushes back

Walls and rocks are solid, using the same contact geometry the bench builds. A block that meets one is stopped, its skin registers the force (the block glows amber), and the rest of the body keeps undulating against it.

Food is a direction

Drop food and a chemical field spreads from it. The two head sensors read it, and their difference bends the wave toward the richer side. The creature does not stop at the food. The bench gives it no halt on arrival.

Is this really the bench?

The bodies are not redrawn for the web. Each model below is the MJCF that smn-lab's own builders produce at commit . The controllers (messaging beam, opponent board) and the two media were ported line for line to JavaScript. We ran each configuration through the Python bench and through this page's engine and compared the trajectories.

The MJCF for every chain body, with or without walls, rocks and skin, is built in the page by a port of build_crawler_xml. Across 73 configurations its output is byte-for-byte identical to the Python builder's. Without contact, the chain creatures match the Python bench to the last bit of double precision. With contact against rocks, the match is as close as the bench can match itself: perturbing the Python run by 10−15 moves it by the same 10−3 m. The bell is chaotic in the same way: over 24 s it rises 0.065 here, against 0.058 to 0.064 across perturbed Python runs (the bench reports +0.061).

One finding for the bench came out of this check. The touch skin's sensing site is exactly the size of the segment's box. A contact at a box corner therefore sits on the site's boundary, and whether it is counted is decided by round-off. Native Python dropped two 0.0096 N corner contacts that the browser counted. Making the site slightly larger than the box would remove the ambiguity.

What this page does not show: entrainment of the rhythm by the body (still an open problem on the ledger), self-models and world-models (the bench measures them, nothing here consumes them), and any claim about real animals. The media are explicit drag and friction laws, not fluid or soil simulations, and every body moves in a plane except the bell.
